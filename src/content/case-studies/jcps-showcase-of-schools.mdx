<Section id="overview" n="01" title="Overview">

Every fall, the Showcase of Schools puts every JCPS school in one place for one day, so families can compare neighborhood schools, magnets and specialized programs face to face. I designed the event brand in 2017 and led its 2018 rebuild across print, digital, broadcast and the convention floor.

<Section id="challenge" n="02" title="The challenge">

Choosing a school is one of the biggest decisions in a child's year. JCPS offers everything from neighborhood schools to specialized magnet programs, and the Showcase is where families meet principals and teachers face to face. Every school arrives with its own look and message, so the event brand has to tie the day together and help parents find their way.

In 2017 I designed the first Showcase identity: a blue and teal look built on shapes from the JCPS logo. It ran on billboards and drew positive feedback.

In 2018, JCPS's new Communications Marketing Manager asked for more. She came from the agency world in California, where she had worked with designers who branded events like South by Southwest (SXSW), the Austin festival known for bold, high-energy music, film and tech branding, and she wanted JCPS Materials Production to work like an in-house brand and marketing agency.

The pressure was real. Local agencies were winning school branding projects, and outside firms were taking high-visibility district work pro bono. Her ask: more color, no JCPS logo shapes in the background, and the energy of a festival.

I was the second-newest designer in a department where most had started in the 1990s and early 2000s, and I saw the brief as an opening. She wanted SXSW, so we delivered.

<Callout label="The question I set out to answer">
How might we make a school-choice event feel like a festival families want to attend, and show that an in-house team can deliver agency-level work?
</Callout>

<Section id="solution" n="03" title="The solution">

One visual system followed families from the first ad they saw to the booth they visited. The big idea was a festival, not a school notice: modern, futuristic and fun, in the spirit of SXSW. A saturated purple-to-orange gradient, fluid shapes and dot grids carried the energy, while the boxed wordmark with its vertical year kept the event recognizable.

<Section id="process" n="04" title="Process">

I led the 2018 rebuild as creative director, project manager and budget manager, compressed into three weeks.

<Steps>
<Step n={1} total={5} title="Discover and plan" img="jcps-step-1" summary="The Showcase moved to the newly renovated Kentucky International Convention Center in 2018. The Showcase moved to the newly renovated Kentucky International Convention Center in 2018. I toured the space with the Communications Marketing Manager before it opened, then set the plan.

- Met with district leaders, school representatives and vendors to understand who the event served
- Set the timeline, deliverables, budget and who owned what

</Step>
<Step n={2} total={5} title="Define the direction" img="jcps-step-2" contain summary="I turned the SXSW brief into a strategy built around one question: how does a parent find their way to the right school?" alt="The 2018 Showcase of Schools style guide: Encode Sans typography, a five-color palette, the gradient background, the logo lockup, fluid shapes, dot grids and a sample layout.">

I turned the SXSW brief into a strategy built around one question: how does a parent find their way to the right school?

- Kept the 2017 boxed wordmark so families would still recognize the event
- Built a style guide for type, color, shapes and layout so every piece matched

</Step>
<Step n={3} total={5} title="Divide and direct" img="jcps-step-3" summary="I split the work by strength and was the go-between for the team and the Communications Marketing Manager." alt="A diagram of the 2018 team: the Communications Marketing Manager gave the brief and feedback to me as Lead Designer, who directed the newest designer, building the assets, and the veteran designer, who owned large-format production.">

I split the work by strength and was the go-between for the team and the Communications Marketing Manager.

- The newest designer built the assets while I gave feedback
- A veteran designer, our large-format expert, owned production of the big pieces

</Step>
<Step n={4} total={5} title="Make and install" img="jcps-step-4" summary="I art-directed every piece across print, digital and the venue, and kept budget and schedule on track through installation." alt="2018 Showcase of Schools print pieces: a poster, a flyer, a phone-number magnet and a postcard, all in the festival gradient.">

I art-directed every piece across print, digital and the venue, and kept budget and schedule on track through installation.

- Rolled the style guide out to posters, postcards, ads, banners and signage
- Worked with vendors and internal teams through production

</Step>
<Step n={5} total={5} title="Measure" img="jcps-step-5" summary="The first Showcase with real attendance data, so future events had a number to beat." alt="A graphic in the 2018 style reading 13,000 attendees at the 2018 Showcase, the first Showcase with real attendance data.">

The first Showcase with real attendance data, so future events had a number to beat.

- Part of the Communications Marketing Manager's more data-driven approach to marketing
- 13,000 attendees gave the district its first real baseline

</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout label="The hardest part: three weeks">
Identity, production and installation all had to fit in three weeks. Splitting roles on day one, with one designer building assets and one owning large format, let all three of us work in parallel.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section id="lasted" n="05" title="A system that lasted">

The system outlasted my time at JCPS. When I left in 2019 for a corporate career, JCPS Materials Production, the in-house team I had worked on, kept the 2018 direction for every Showcase through 2021.

In 2022 the team moved the Showcase to the official JCPS brand colors, a sound call for district brand consistency. They kept the playful elements: the boxed wordmark with its vertical year, the dot grids and the fluid shapes. The 2026 materials still use them, and the team has extended the system with new pieces such as a multilingual welcome banner.
