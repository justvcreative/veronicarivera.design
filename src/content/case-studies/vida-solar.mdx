<Section id="overview" n="01" title="Overview">

Commissioning is the step that connects a new solar plant's trackers to the systems that monitor and control them. Technicians did it by juggling Excel files and raw database uploads. I designed an internal app that walks them through setup one step at a time, so trackers get connected with fewer errors and less guesswork.

<Section id="challenge" n="02" title="The challenge">

Commissioning a solar plant meant translating dense engineering data by hand. Technicians worked across multiple Excel spreadsheets, then uploaded the results straight into a database. Every handoff was a chance for a typo, and every error cost time in the field.

The data itself was hard to read. Technicians had to interpret plant architecture, tracker configurations and site conditions without a tool built for how they work. The people doing the most critical setup step had the least support.

<Callout label="The question I set out to answer">
How might we give technicians one clear path through commissioning, so accuracy comes from the tool rather than from memory and double-checking?
</Callout>

</Figure>

</Section>

<Section id="process" n="04" title="Process">

I started with the technicians, not the screens. Before designing anything, I asked how they commissioned a plant today and collected their real spreadsheets, files and manuals.

<Steps>
<Step n={1} total={4} title="Discovery" img="solar-step-1" summary="I ran weekly sessions with client stakeholders to map the full commissioning process." alt="A diagram of commissioning as it was: engineering data translated by hand into Excel spreadsheets, uploaded raw into the database, and errors found in the field sending work back for rework.">

I ran weekly sessions with client stakeholders to map the full commissioning process.

- Gathered technical requirements, field terminology and how technicians prefer to see data
- Learned how the back end connects to the interface, so the design respected real system constraints
- Mapped it all in Miro, so stakeholders could review on their own time

</Step>
<Step n={2} total={4} title="Wireframes and prototypes" img="solar-step-2" summary="I turned those requirements into wireframes, then an interactive Figma prototype. A key requirement: design for the large, company-issued iPads technicians use in the field." alt="The app's upload screen on an iPad in perspective, against a sunrise gradient.">

I turned those requirements into wireframes, then an interactive Figma prototype. A key requirement: design for the large, company-issued iPads technicians use in the field.

- A large iPad has room for a desktop-style layout, so technicians keep the side navigation and full data tables in view
- But they work by touch, not with a mouse, so I sized buttons, table rows and controls for fingers and planned for touch gestures
- Reviewed with stakeholders at each stage, rather than saving feedback for the end

</Step>
<Step n={3} total={4} title="Feedback and iteration" img="solar-step-3" summary="I kept gathering input from stakeholders and technicians through design and development." alt="The File Upload component spec: its anatomy, five states from default to error, the loading animation in CSS and specs for engineers.">

I kept gathering input from stakeholders and technicians through design and development.

- Each round sharpened usability and kept the tool aligned with field needs
- A design system let us spin up testable MVP screens quickly
- Documented every component's anatomy, states and specs, so the client and engineers could explore the system themselves and build exactly what was intended

</Step>
<Step n={4} total={4} title="Handoff and build support" img="solar-step-4" summary="I handed the designs to developers and stayed on through implementation." alt="Plant Configuration screens in Figma, with annotations, empty states and error states.">

I handed the designs to developers and stayed on through implementation.

- Answered design questions as they came up
- Checked the build against stakeholder and user expectations

</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout>
Speed was my biggest strength on this project. I got prototypes running quickly and reworked them as soon as new information surfaced, so stakeholders were always reacting to something real.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section id="impact" n="05" title="Impact">

The app replaced a spreadsheet-and-upload process with one guided tool, so technicians spend their time commissioning rather than reconciling files. It also gave the client remote visibility into tracker performance across its global sites.
